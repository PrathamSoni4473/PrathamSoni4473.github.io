Beer Portfolio
Optimisation.

A US national distributor: 150 products, 7 regions, 5 channels, −0.4% revenue growth over two years. A Power BI narrative that unpacked why - and quantified an $80k profit lever hiding inside the discount budget.

Course
UNSW · INFS5700
Year
2026
Tools
Power BI, DAX, Python (EDA)
Role
Analyst & storyteller

01The problem

A national US beer distributor with 150 products across 7 regions and 5 sales channels had flat revenue growth of −0.4% over two years. Management had no clear view of which products were actually driving profitability versus volume, which regions were shifting in their preferences, or whether decisions around discounting and channel mix were helping or hurting the business.

The brief reduced to one deceptively simple question: how should the company optimise its beer portfolio?

02Approach

The analysis was structured around the four analytics types - descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, prescriptive - so every finding built logically toward a recommendation rather than landing as a disconnected data dump.

  • Started with EDA in Python before touching Power BI - verifying distributions, YoY growth by family and region, BCG classification, stress-testing every claim before it became a slide
  • Built a star-schema model in Power BI on top of 11,000 transactions, with DAX measures for revenue, profit, margin, YoY growth, discount impact, and BCG class
  • Authored the dashboard as a five-page narrative - each page answering one question and building tension toward the recommendations on page five

"Top-selling products had zero overlap with top-margin products" - the single sentence that re-framed the entire portfolio strategy conversation.

1 - Volume and profitability are telling different stories

The company was celebrating and investing in busy products, not profitable ones.

2 - Six out of ten families are shrinking

Only Porter (+17.3%), IPA (+9.6%) and Wheat (+2.4%) are growing. Strong Ale lost 25.1% of revenue in a single year - declining in six out of seven regions.

3 - Discount and channel choice are the same problem

Bar generates more revenue than high-discount Supermarket without giving a single dollar away in discounts. The discount line item and the channel mix line item were, in effect, the same lever pulled twice.

4 - Regional demand is shifting and being ignored

Wheat doubled in the Mountain region (+100.1%). IPA is surging on the West Coast (+26.8%). The Southeast is transitioning from Ale (−26.1%) to IPA (+13.4%). The data showed exactly where demand was heading; the portfolio had not responded.

5 - 37 products are classified as Dogs

Together they generate $301,215 in profit - less than half what the 37 Stars generate ($604,457) from a similar number of products.

04Recommendations

  • Promote 14 product–region–channel combinations - the highest-rated product in each region's two fastest-growing families, pushed through Bar & Restaurant where margins are highest
  • Reposition 3 products (8 Ball Stout, Bobo's Robust Porter, Over Ale) currently over-reliant on Supermarket - margin gap of +7.9% to +10.7% by switching channels
  • Discontinue 7 products: 2 immediately (Orion Southern Star, Purple Haze - review < 3.5, declining, near-zero contribution) and 5 phased out over 12 months (incl. Buffalo Bill's Stout −45% YoY, Belzebuth −34.3% YoY)
  • Redirect the freed revenue to Stars - unlocking an additional $9,920 in profit on top of the $80k channel reallocation

05Limitations

Acknowledging where the data ends and judgement begins - because a credible recommendation is one that names its own caveats.

⚠ ELASTICITY

No demand-elasticity data

$80,234 is a theoretical upper bound. Customers who only buy through Supermarket discount may not follow a product to Bar - actual gain depends on volume retention through the channel shift.

⚠ DIRECTION

Correlation isn't causation

Review scores correlate with revenue (r = 0.60), but bad reviews may be a consequence of bad placement. Some products flagged for discontinuation may actually be reposition candidates we missed.

⚠ EXECUTION

Regional pilots are directional

Mountain Wheat at +100% is a real signal, but capturing it depends on distributor relationships, shelf access and local competition the dataset doesn't see.

⚠ FRAMING

BCG thresholds are relative

Using portfolio median as the threshold means a "Dog" here might be a "Star" in a smaller portfolio. The classification is valid for prioritisation, not absolute judgement.

06What it taught me

Technically

How to build a proper star schema, write filter-context-aware DAX, and use Python for EDA before committing to a visual narrative. The discipline of verifying every number from the raw data - rather than trusting the tool - carries forward into every project.

Analytically

The difference between describing data and diagnosing it. The zero-overlap finding came from asking the right question, not running the right chart. Always challenge the obvious interpretation before accepting it.

As a storyteller

A dashboard nobody acts on is worthless, regardless of how technically correct it is. Structuring the analysis as a five-page narrative - tension, evidence, resolution - made the recommendations feel inevitable rather than arbitrary.

About limitations

Acknowledging what the data doesn't know is analytical maturity, not weakness. Management trusts an analyst more when they say "here is where the data ends and judgement begins."
