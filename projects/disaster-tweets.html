Disaster Tweet
Classification.

53,531 tweets, four embedding spaces, three architectures - and a hybrid ensemble that lifted accuracy to 89.12%, beating every individual model in the bake-off.

Read More about the publication here.

Year
Oct 2023
Source
CrisisNLP · 53,531 tweets
Stack
BERT · XLNet · GloVe · FastText · LSTM
Result
89.12% accuracy (ensemble)

01The problem

Twitter is a real-time signal during natural disasters - but most of what's said about a disaster isn't actually about one. Sarcasm, news commentary, meme references, and metaphorical use of disaster vocabulary swamp the genuine eyewitness reports. Keyword filters fail; emergency responders need a model that reads context, not just lexicon.

02Approach

A four-phase framework from raw tweets to ensembled prediction:

  • Data & preprocessing. 53,531 tweets sourced from CrisisNLP. Cleaned URLs / hashtags / punctuation, lemmatised, converted emojis into textual representations
  • Embeddings. Four numerical-representation techniques: BERT (transformer), XLNet (autoregressive), GloVe (co-occurrence matrix), FastText (subword)
  • Base architectures. Simple feed-forward, CNN, and LSTM heads on top of each embedding
  • Hybrid ensemble. The final model combined BERT + FastText + GloVe with an LSTM head and averaged predictions to capture a wider range of semantic and contextual detail

Each embedding gets a different slice of the signal wrong. Ensembling isn't a shortcut - it's a structural answer to disagreement.

Across architectures, BERT and FastText consistently outperformed GloVe and XLNet - BERT for contextual nuance, FastText for morphological detail and out-of-vocabulary robustness on noisy social-media text.

04Why it matters

Accurate classification of disaster tweets contributes directly to real-time emergency response and decision-making. By filtering out the irrelevant noise, the model lets responders use social media for coordination and situational awareness during a crisis - minutes instead of hours of triage.

05What it taught me

Hybrids are robust

Combining multiple embeddings via a hybrid model captured a broader semantic spectrum than any single representation could - and the ensemble was less brittle on edge cases than the strongest individual model.

LSTM fits the medium

LSTM is well-suited to disaster tweets because it captures sequential dependencies and temporal information - essential for short, context-laden social-media text.

Context vs morphology

BERT excels at context; FastText excels at out-of-vocabulary words and morphological variants. Picking either alone leaves accuracy on the table - which is exactly what the ensemble exploits.

Filter, don't replace

The framing was always to surface candidates for a human triage operator, not to autonomously dispatch responders. The right metric mix follows from the right framing.
