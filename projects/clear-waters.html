Clear Waters:
Plastic, detected.

A full-stack platform - drone, mobile, web - that detects plastic in rivers, maps the hotspots, and predicts where the trash will drift next. Built with Mano for the REVA × Kyndryl hackathon.

Context
REVA × Kyndryl Hackathon

01The problem

Plastic pollution in rivers is a growing environmental crisis, and there is no scalable, automated way to detect, monitor, and predict the location of plastic waste in waterways. Cleanup is slow, reactive, and resource-intensive - crews show up after a complaint, after a flood, after the trash has already drifted downstream.

The platform tries to flip that into something proactive: watch the water for you, flag plastic in near-real-time, and forecast where it's heading.

03The pipeline

Five stages, end-to-end, from a frame on the water to a circle on a map.

STEP 01

Image capture & upload

Images come in via drone, Flutter mobile app (camera or gallery), or bulk Dropbox upload. The source is detected from image metadata so the right processing pipeline kicks in automatically.

STEP 02

Pre-processing

Glare removal eliminates white reflections from the water surface. Segmentation masks out green land areas so the model only sees plastic in water. Applied to drone images only - detected via metadata.

STEP 03

Dual YOLO detection

Model 1 (YOLOv8x) trained on 2,800 Saigon River drone frames - top-down views, mostly white plastics. Model 2 (YOLOv8n) trained on 6,523 diverse global plastic images (various angles & colours), reduced from 25,000 using VGG16 + PCA + K-Means clustering into 40 clusters. Results merged via DBSCAN, keeping the highest-confidence detections.

STEP 04

Storage & API layer

FastAPI handles real-time image processing and data retrieval. All images, counts, locations and metadata are stored in the user's personal Dropbox - structured folders + Excel sheets - with OAuth 2.0 tokens managing secure session handling.

STEP 05

Location & drift prediction

GPS metadata generates Google Maps links. A time-slider lets users predict future plastic drift using wind speed, wind direction, tide height, and wave period. Predicted points are snapped to the nearest water body using Vietnam geospatial data.

05The outcome

A full-stack platform combining a web dashboard, mobile app and ML pipeline that detects, maps, predicts, and historically tracks plastic pollution in rivers.

  • Interactive map - visual hotspots with DBSCAN clustering, directions to cleanup sites, and a drift-prediction slider
  • History & stats - full detection log in Dropbox with timestamps, counts, lat/long and image links; Power BI dashboard for trends
  • Mobile app - Flutter, for field workers - upload images, see plastic counts instantly, jump to the location on Google Maps
  • Scalable pipeline - handles single uploads or bulk drone batches via scheduled API runs; stationary vs moving plastic classified separately
The judges said the explanation module was what set us apart - accuracy gets you to the demo; trust gets you adopted.

06What it taught me

Two models, one decision

Single-model pipelines fail on edge cases the training set didn't see. Pairing a domain-specialist (drone, top-down) with a generalist (varied angles & colours) and merging via DBSCAN was the cleanest way to get coverage without retraining one giant model.

Ship the explanation

Hackathons reward shipping, not architecture. We deliberately kept the vision model lean and put the polish into the explanation UI, the map, and the forecast overlay - decision-makers don't read loss curves; they read circles on a map.

Data > model

Reducing 25,000 noisy images to 6,523 curated ones via clustering bought more accuracy than any architectural tweak we tried in Phase 3.

Shipping is the deadline

End-to-end - drone footage to a cleanup-team app - is harder than any one of its parts. The integration work (FastAPI ↔ Dropbox ↔ Flutter) was where most of the wins, and most of the bugs, actually lived.
