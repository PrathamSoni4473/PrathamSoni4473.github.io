01Context

Silver Oaks sells BIS Hallmarked pure 925 silver - jewellery, idols, home decor, and bespoke gifting across five in-house collections. The founder came in with a clear brand identity and a specific ask: a store they could run themselves, without being locked into a platform they didn't control.

The brief covered a full product catalogue, a custom admin panel, order management, and email notifications - scoped across three tiers depending on how much automation made sense at launch.

02The website

The customer side was built around the five Silver Oaks collections - Me'Ora, Ishwara, Silvora, Star Jewel, and World of Silver - with product listing, detail pages, cart, checkout, and UPI-based order confirmation. Mobile-first throughout.

NOTE: THE SITE IS STILL IN DEVELOPMENT.

03The admin panel

The part most clients underestimate. Every product, brand, order, and customer is manageable from a secure panel - no FTP, no database client, no calling the developer.

  • Add, edit, delete products and brands with image uploads
  • View and update orders, add tracking numbers manually
  • Customer management - view order history, mark active or inactive
  • CMS controls for the About and Contact pages

Scoped so the client is fully self-sufficient from day one.

04Scope and decisions

The project was quoted in three tiers - Basic (manual UPI, core store), Standard (customer accounts, search, filters, SEO), and Full (Razorpay, Delhivery API, coupon system). A clear scope boundary was set before development: anything outside the signed tier is a separate quote, not a surprise mid-sprint.

That structure protects both sides - the client knows exactly what they're getting, and the build doesn't sprawl.

05What it taught me

Scope is a design decision

Writing the tier breakdown before touching code forced every feature to earn its place. The client knew what they were paying for at each level, which made conversations easier and scope creep a non-issue.

Admin UX matters as much as the storefront

The customer-facing pages are what the client shows people. The admin panel is what they use every single day. Spending time on that interface isn't overhead - it's the product.
