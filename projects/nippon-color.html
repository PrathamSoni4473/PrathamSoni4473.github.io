01Context

Two threads of work that ended up reinforcing each other - a website rebuild that gave us cleaner traffic to measure, and a Power BI layer that turned that traffic into decisions about merchandising and conversion.

Specifics of the internal dashboard and backend work stay with the company. What follows is the public-shaped version.

02The website

Redesigned the corporate site's information architecture and front-end - faster, lighter, and easier to navigate on mobile. Conducted end-to-end testing and bug resolution to keep the experience consistent across devices, alsa available at nipponcolor.com.

03The dashboards

Built Power BI dashboards to monitor daily traffic, conversion funnels, and revenue. The work was less about the charts and more about the questions the team could finally ask: where do users drop off, what does that cost us, which page is the leak?

  • Daily traffic + funnel views - drop-off visible at a glance, not buried in a report
  • Revenue attribution by acquisition channel and product line
  • Operational reports stakeholders could open themselves, instead of asking for a refresh

Specifics of the model and visuals are confidential - happy to walk through the structure in conversation.

04Working with the team

Cross-functional sprints with marketing, design and engineering - aligning deliverables, resolving blockers, accelerating timelines. First time I properly owned a thread inside a real Agile cycle, and the first time stakeholders pointed at a dashboard I built when defending a decision in a meeting.

05What it taught me

Build the lens, not just the page

The website redesign felt like the marquee deliverable - but the BI layer was the bit that actually changed how the team made calls week to week.

Self-serve beats bespoke

The most-used reports were the ones stakeholders could open themselves. Bespoke pulls bottleneck on the analyst; templates scale.
