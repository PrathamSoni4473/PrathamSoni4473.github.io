Forecasting
Mumbai's air.

A hybrid ensemble that decomposes the signal first and predicts what's left - tested on 3+ years of multivariate AQI / PM2.5 / PM10 data from six Mumbai monitoring stations, beating VAR, VARMA, and CEEMDAN baselines.

Read More about the publication here.

Year
Sept 2023
Stack
Python · STL · CNN-LSTM · RNN
Targets
AQI · PM2.5 · PM10
Validation
RMSE · MAPE · R²

01The problem

Air pollution is a critical public-health threat in densely populated cities like Mumbai - whose air quality has at times surpassed Delhi's. Traditional statistical methods (Vector Autoregression, VAR) struggle to forecast it accurately because the signal is highly non-linear and tangled with complex temporal patterns.

02Approach

Multivariate time-series data - AQI, PM2.5, PM10 - from six Mumbai monitoring stations, January 2020 through May 2023. To wrestle the non-linearity, the series was decomposed using STL (Seasonal and Trend decomposition using Loess) into trend, seasonal, and residual components - and then specialist learners were trained per component.

  • Trend model: CNN + LSTM stack - convolutional layers extract local patterns, LSTM captures long-range dependence
  • Seasonal & residual models: stacked RNN layers + Dense heads - good fit for periodic and short-cycle dynamics
  • Forecasts from the three component models are recombined via ensemble aggregation into a single AQI / PM2.5 / PM10 prediction
  • Validated against VAR, VARMA, and CEEMDAN baselines on RMSE, MAPE, R²

03Findings

Decomposition isn't preprocessing - it's giving each learner a problem it knows how to solve.

The STL-based ensemble outperformed VAR, VARMA, and CEEMDAN-decomposed alternatives on every target. Trend, seasonal, and residual components were each well-fit and generalised cleanly to held-out test data.

04Why it matters

Reliable short-horizon AQI forecasts give policymakers and environmental agencies a real lever: school-day advisories, traffic-flow nudges, construction-site shutdown thresholds. The work was framed throughout as a decision-support tool, not a leaderboard exercise.

05What it taught me

Decomposition is the lever

Breaking complex series into trend / seasonal / residual reduced prediction error far more than swapping in a fancier single model on raw data.

Match the architecture to the component

CNN-LSTM for trends, stacked RNNs for seasonality. Forcing one architecture across all components is leaving accuracy on the table.

Pre-decomposition lessons mislead

RNNs looked dominant on raw data - but post-decomposition, the hybrid ensemble was the most accurate final solution. Don't pick the model before you understand the signal.

Report two metrics, always

Pair R² with RMSE so non-statisticians can read the chart. R² alone hides bias; RMSE alone hides variance explained.
